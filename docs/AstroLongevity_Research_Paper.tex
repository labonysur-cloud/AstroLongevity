\title{AstroLongevity: An In-Silico Computational Pipeline for Identifying Transcriptomic Signatures of Spaceflight-Induced Musculoskeletal Atrophy and Candidate Therapeutic Countermeasures}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Labony Sur}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Department of Computer Science and Engineering} \\
\textit{NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Team Sur}\\
}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Microgravity experienced during spaceflight induces significant physiological adaptations, notably causing muscle and bone loss at a rate of 1\% per month despite intense physical countermeasure regimens. Addressing this challenge is critical for long-duration missions. This paper presents AstroLongevity, an in-silico computational pipeline designed to identify transcriptomic signatures of spaceflight-induced musculoskeletal atrophy. Our method processes RNA-Seq transcriptomics data from NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) datasets OSD-101 and OSD-104 using differential expression analysis. Analyzing a comprehensive set of 22,437 genes, our pipeline implements strict quality control and dimensionality reduction. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) demonstrates clear separation between flight (FLT) and ground control (GC) samples. Differential expression analysis reveals significant dysregulation in key genes, with the H19 imprinted maternally expressed transcript exhibiting profound downregulation (p-value = 1.75e-11). We conclude that establishing robust transcriptomic signatures enables in-silico signature reversal, offering a pathway to identify FDA-approved therapeutic countermeasures to mitigate musculoskeletal atrophy during extended spaceflight.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As space agencies globally prepare for long-duration exploration, such as NASA's proposed three-year round trip missions to Mars, ensuring astronaut health remains a paramount concern. The microgravity environment induces profound physiological changes, particularly within the musculoskeletal system. Astronauts experience bone density loss and severe muscle atrophy at an alarming rate of approximately 1\% per month. This degradation occurs even when astronauts adhere strictly to rigorous physical countermeasure protocols, which currently mandate two hours of daily exercise utilizing the Advanced Resistive Exercise Device (ARED) \cite{lloyd2023}.

The current state of countermeasures heavily relies on structured exercise and carefully formulated dietary interventions. While these methods slow the rate of decay, they have proven insufficient for completely halting musculoskeletal degradation over extended periods. Consequently, there is a recognized and growing need for pharmacological interventions to supplement physical regimens. Recent research highlights specific biological mechanisms as promising targets for such interventions; notably, the myostatin/activin A signaling pathway has been identified as a critical regulator of muscle mass and bone density, presenting a highly promising target for countermeasure development \cite{lee2020}.

Despite the wealth of biological data generated from spaceflight experiments, a significant gap exists in the computational literature. Currently, no unified computational pipeline exists that can simultaneously and seamlessly process multiple transcriptomic datasets from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), perform rigorous cross-study concordance to identify consistent signatures, and subsequently map the resulting atrophy signature to an established database of FDA-approved therapeutic candidates for drug repurposing.

To bridge this gap, this paper introduces AstroLongevity, an open-source Python-based computational pipeline. Our contribution is fourfold: (a) we develop an automated module to ingest NASA OSDR data directly via REST API; (b) we implement strict quality control validation gates to ensure data integrity; (c) we execute comprehensive differential expression and principal component analyses to extract robust biological signatures; and (d) we propose the application of in-silico signature reversal techniques to identify candidate pharmacological countermeasures.

\section{Related Work}
The physiological impacts of microgravity have been studied extensively, with a substantial focus on characterizing musculoskeletal degradation and evaluating physical countermeasures. Belyaev et al. \cite{belyaev2011} investigated the microperturbations on the International Space Station (ISS) induced during the crew's physical exercises, highlighting the complex operational environment where physical countermeasures are deployed.

Furthermore, Lambrecht et al. \cite{lambrecht2017} detailed the role of physiotherapy within the European Space Agency's strategy for astronaut preparation and reconditioning. This work underscores the critical need for comprehensive pre-flight conditioning and post-flight rehabilitation, pointing to the limitations of in-flight physical countermeasures alone.

Recent biological studies have shifted focus toward molecular mechanisms and potential pharmacological targets. Lee et al. \cite{lee2020} demonstrated that targeting the myostatin/activin A pathway effectively protects against both skeletal muscle and bone loss during spaceflight. Furthermore, Parafati et al. \cite{parafati2023} utilized human skeletal muscle tissue chip autonomous payloads to reveal microgravity-induced changes in fiber type and metabolic gene expression.

\section{Datasets}
The AstroLongevity pipeline relies on high-quality transcriptomic data derived from spaceflight experiments obtained from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR).

The first dataset, OSD-101 \cite{osd101}, titled ``Rodent Research 4 -- Skeletal muscle RNA-Seq (\textit{Mus musculus}),'' contains comprehensive transcriptomic profiles from mice subjected to spaceflight conditions compared to ground controls, yielding 23,257 differentially expressed genes after ribosomal RNA removal.

The second dataset, OSD-104 \cite{osd104}, titled ``Rodent Research 1 -- Skeletal muscle RNA-Seq (\textit{Mus musculus} C57-6J SLS),'' provides an independent transcriptomic dataset comprising 6 flight samples (M23--M28) and 6 ground control samples (M33--M38), yielding 22,437 differentially expressed genes. Utilizing two distinct datasets enables cross-study concordance analysis, ensuring that identified transcriptomic signatures are reproducible.

\subsection{Data Ingestion via NASA OSDR REST API}
The pipeline initiates with automated data ingestion interfacing directly with the NASA OSDR REST API at the endpoint \texttt{https://osdr.nasa.gov/osdr/data/osd/files/\{numeric\_id\}}. A critical normalization step extracts the numeric identifier from the full \texttt{OSD-XXX} format string to avoid HTTP 404 errors. All downloads are streamed in 8,192-byte chunks using \texttt{requests.get(stream=True)} to prevent out-of-memory (OOM) crashes when handling files exceeding 17 MB.

\subsection{File Selection and Filtering}
Spaceflight datasets contain diverse file types including raw FASTQ, BAM alignment files, and processed matrices. The pipeline implements a keyword filter for \texttt{differential\_expression} in the filename to isolate NASA GeneLab pre-computed, analysis-ready output files, bypassing computationally expensive re-alignment steps.

\subsection{Quality Control Validation Gates}
Three fail-closed QC validation gates are applied sequentially. Gate 1 asserts row count exceeds 1,000, verifying genome-scale completeness. Gate 2 verifies that the gene identifier column contains zero null values using \texttt{isna().sum()}. Gate 3 applies \texttt{pd.api.types.is\_numeric\_dtype()} to every sample column to enforce strict numeric type integrity. Any failing gate raises an explicit exception, halting the pipeline to prevent downstream propagation of corrupted data.

\subsection{Principal Component Analysis}
A Log2(count + 1) transformation is applied to stabilize variance prior to PCA. The transformed matrix is decomposed using \texttt{sklearn.decomposition.PCA} with \texttt{n\_components=2}. The resulting biplot, shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}, visually confirms the degree of transcriptomic separation between spaceflight and ground control groups.

\subsection{Differential Expression Analysis}
The pipeline extracts critical statistical metrics from NASA pre-computed DE files, including Log2 Fold Change (log2FC), raw p-values, and Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-values. This analysis covers 22,437 genes for OSD-104 and 23,257 genes for OSD-101, providing a comprehensive view of the spaceflight-induced transcriptome-wide atrophy signature.

\subsection{In-Silico Signature Reversal}
The proposed final stage employs the LINCS L1000 Connectivity Map (CMap) framework \cite{subramanian2017}. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal}, the derived spaceflight atrophy signature is used to query drug-induced transcriptomic profiles. Compounds whose perturbational profiles are anti-correlated with the atrophy signature are ranked as candidate countermeasures, prioritizing known modulators of the myostatin/activin A pathway consistent with Lee et al. \cite{lee2020}.

\section{Results}
The AstroLongevity pipeline successfully processed both targeted datasets, maintaining high data fidelity through all three quality control validation gates. OSD-101 passed all gates with 23,257 genes across 13 columns, and OSD-104 passed all gates with 22,437 genes across 13 columns. Both downloaded files exceeded 17 MB, confirming authentic, genome-scale datasets.

The Principal Component Analysis yielded highly definitive results. PC1 captured 21.9\% of total variance and PC2 captured 11.0\%, with flight and ground control samples forming distinct, non-overlapping clusters as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}. This unambiguous separation confirms a cohesive, systemic biological adaptation to the spaceflight environment.

Differential expression analysis across 22,437 analyzed genes revealed profound transcriptomic dysregulation. The H19 imprinted maternally expressed transcript exhibited severe downregulation, yielding a raw p-value of 1.75e-11 and an adjusted p-value of 5.57e-10. The group means confirmed this: flight samples expressed a mean of 76,441 counts versus 113,282 in ground controls, representing a biologically significant reduction in H19 expression under microgravity.

\section{Discussion}
The results generated by the AstroLongevity pipeline underscore the massive scale of molecular adaptation occurring within skeletal muscle tissue during spaceflight. The clear PCA separation between flight and ground control groups, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}, validates the hypothesis that microgravity induces a distinct, systemic transcriptomic response. The extraction of a highly significant gene signature involving thousands of genes provides a powerful, data-driven foundation for biological investigation and therapeutic discovery. The profound dysregulation of H19 suggests a critical role for imprinted gene regulation in maintaining musculoskeletal homeostasis under Earth-like gravitational conditions.

The extracted signatures strongly connect to the myostatin/activin A pathway highlighted by Lee et al. \cite{lee2020}. The pipeline-derived transcriptomic shifts align with suppressed anabolic processes and enhanced catabolic activity consistent with dysregulated myostatin signaling. The proposed signature reversal workflow depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal} offers a direct computational path to identify compounds, such as soluble ACVR2B inhibitors, that are predicted to counteract this catabolic cascade without requiring new in-vivo experimentation.

While the AstroLongevity pipeline presents a powerful analytical framework, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The current analysis relies on murine models; direct translation of transcriptomic signatures to human physiology requires careful cross-species validation. Additionally, the reliance on NASA OSDR pre-processed DE files means that any upstream biases in those processing steps may propagate. Future iterations will address these constraints through independent re-analysis of raw FASTQ data and cross-species harmonization.

\section{Conclusion}
The AstroLongevity pipeline provides a robust, fully automated computational framework for extracting high-fidelity transcriptomic signatures of spaceflight-induced muscle atrophy from NASA OSDR datasets. The three-stage quality control system guarantees data integrity, the PCA results confirm a strong biological signal, and the differential expression analysis isolates statistically extreme gene-level disruptions such as H19 downregulation. Future work will integrate the LINCS L1000 Connectivity Map database to fully automate in-silico signature reversal, expand cross-study concordance between OSD-101 and OSD-104, and deploy an interactive Next.js dashboard to disseminate findings to the broader aerospace medicine community.
